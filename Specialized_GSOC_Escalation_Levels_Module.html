The thing to remember

Levels are categories of action. You don't climb them in order, and more than one can be live at the same time.

A Level 3 dispatch has Level 2 running beside it. A fire goes straight to Level 5, and Level 6 can stack on top by morning. When you escalate, say it out loud: "Level 5, Level 2 in parallel."

The two rules over all six

Rule 1 · Life safety

A life-safety event goes straight to Level 5. You never work your way up to it, and you never wait for the Supervisor to answer before you make that call.

Rule 2 · Failed escalations

If an escalation can't be completed because nobody answers or the channel's down, that failure gets escalated one level up right away. An unanswered call doesn't count as a discharged obligation.

Carry these onto the floor

  • Say the level out loud. "Level 3, Level 2 in parallel." If you can't name it, that's a Level 2 by itself.
  • Life safety skips the line. Person down, fire, weapon, an officer who's gone quiet. Level 5 first, everyone else in parallel.
  • Unsure goes up. It's never a disposition. Level 2 is where unsure goes.
  • An unanswered call isn't done. A dead phone or a dead network gets escalated one level up, right away, with every attempt timed in the record.
  • Know whose call it is. Level 4 is the Supervisor's. Level 6 is the Program Director's. You get it to them with the facts ready.
